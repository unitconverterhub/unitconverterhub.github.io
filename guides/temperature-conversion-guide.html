Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin: The Complete Temperature Conversion Guide

UnitConverter guides · Measurement

Temperature is the only everyday measurement where the two main systems do not share a zero point, which is why converting between Celsius and Fahrenheit feels harder than converting length or weight. Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius but 32 degrees Fahrenheit, and it boils at 100 degrees Celsius but 212 degrees Fahrenheit. That offset of 32 degrees is the source of almost every temperature conversion error. Add Kelvin, the scientific scale that starts at absolute zero, and you have three systems that every student, cook, traveller and engineer needs to move between.

How the three scales were defined

The Celsius scale was designed around water: 0 degrees is the freezing point and 100 degrees is the boiling point at standard atmospheric pressure. It was proposed by the Swedish astronomer Anders Celsius in 1742 and is now the standard almost everywhere in the world. The Fahrenheit scale is older, proposed by Daniel Fahrenheit in 1724, and was built around a brine solution and the human body. On his scale, water freezes at 32 degrees and boils at 212 degrees, which gives 180 degrees between freezing and boiling instead of Celsius's neat 100.

Kelvin is different from both: it is an absolute scale that starts at absolute zero, the lowest temperature physically possible, where all molecular motion stops. Zero kelvin is minus 273.15 degrees Celsius, and each kelvin is exactly the same size as one degree Celsius, so converting between Celsius and Kelvin is just a matter of adding or subtracting 273.15. Scientists use Kelvin because many physical laws become simpler when temperature is measured from absolute zero rather than from the freezing point of water.

The conversion formulas

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply by nine, divide by five, and add 32. To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius, subtract 32, multiply by five and divide by nine. These two formulas are exact and work for every temperature. The quick mental version is simpler: double the Celsius temperature and add 30 to get an approximate Fahrenheit value — 20 degrees Celsius doubles to 40, plus 30 gives 70, close to the exact 68 degrees Fahrenheit. The approximation is good enough for weather but not for cooking or science.

For Kelvin, add 273.15 to a Celsius temperature to get kelvin, and subtract 273.15 to go back. There is no degree symbol with kelvin — it is simply 273 kelvin, not 273 degrees kelvin. Room temperature is about 20 degrees Celsius, 68 degrees Fahrenheit and 293 kelvin. A comfortable oven temperature of 180 degrees Celsius is 350 degrees Fahrenheit and 453 kelvin. A fever of 38 degrees Celsius is 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit, which is why thermometers in different countries can look alarming until you convert them.

Everyday reference points

The easiest way to learn temperature conversion is to memorise a handful of anchor points. Water freezes at 0 Celsius and 32 Fahrenheit. A cold winter day might be minus 10 Celsius, which is 14 Fahrenheit. A mild spring day is 15 Celsius or 59 Fahrenheit. A warm summer day is 25 Celsius or 77 Fahrenheit, and a hot day is 30 Celsius or 86 Fahrenheit. Human body temperature is 37 Celsius or 98.6 Fahrenheit. A hot oven is 200 Celsius or 400 Fahrenheit, and a pizza oven can reach 300 Celsius or 570 Fahrenheit.

Temperature in cooking and science

Cooking is where temperature conversion errors have the most delicious consequences. A recipe that says bake at 350 Fahrenheit means 177 Celsius, which most ovens round to 180. If you mistakenly set your oven to 350 Celsius, you will incinerate the dish. Gas ovens add another system entirely: gas mark 4 is about 180 Celsius or 350 Fahrenheit, and each gas mark is roughly 25 Fahrenheit apart. Sugar work is especially sensitive — the soft-ball stage is 115 Celsius, the hard-crack stage is 150 Celsius, and a few degrees either way changes the result completely.

In science, temperature must be converted with care because many formulas only work in kelvin. The ideal gas law, for example, gives nonsense answers if you insert a Celsius temperature. Climate scientists report changes in Celsius because the numbers are small and intuitive, while astronomers use kelvin because stellar temperatures run into the thousands. Whenever you see a temperature in a scientific paper, check the unit before using the number — a temperature of 300 means a pleasant day in kelvin and an impossible one in Celsius.

Frequently asked questions

What is 20 Celsius in Fahrenheit?
20 degrees Celsius is 68 degrees Fahrenheit.

What is 0 Kelvin in Celsius?
0 kelvin is minus 273.15 degrees Celsius — absolute zero.

Is minus 40 the same in both scales?
Yes — minus 40 degrees Celsius equals minus 40 degrees Fahrenheit.
