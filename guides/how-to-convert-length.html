How to Convert Length Units Accurately

UnitConverter guides · Measurement

Length is the measurement we convert most often: checking a person's height, reading a map, buying curtains or following a recipe that uses a different system from our own. Getting it right matters more than it seems — a curtain cut two inches short cannot be fixed, and a shelf measured in the wrong unit will not fit the wall. The good news is that length conversion is simple arithmetic once you know the exact factors, and there are reliable shortcuts for quick estimates when precision does not matter.

The exact conversion factors

Every length conversion starts from a small set of exact definitions. One inch is defined as exactly 25.4 millimetres, and everything else follows from that single fact. One foot is twelve inches, so it equals exactly 30.48 centimetres. One yard is three feet, or 0.9144 metres. One mile is 5,280 feet, which works out to exactly 1,609.344 metres. On the metric side, the relationships are decimal: ten millimetres make a centimetre, one hundred centimetres make a metre, and one thousand metres make a kilometre.

Because the inch is defined exactly, conversions between imperial and metric length can be done with perfect precision. Multiply inches by 2.54 to get centimetres, divide centimetres by 2.54 to get inches, multiply miles by 1.609344 to get kilometres, and multiply kilometres by 0.621371 to get miles. For everyday use, rounded factors such as 2.5 centimetres per inch or 1.6 kilometres per mile are close enough, but for engineering, medicine or anything where a small error could cause harm, always use the exact factor or a trusted converter.

Quick mental shortcuts

When you need a rough answer in your head, a few approximations will serve you well. An inch is about two and a half centimetres, so a ten-inch screen is roughly twenty-five centimetres across. A foot is about thirty centimetres, so a six-foot person is about 183 centimetres tall. A yard is a little under a metre — about ninety-one centimetres — so three feet of fabric is just short of a metre. A mile is about one and a half kilometres, which makes it easy to estimate driving distances: sixty miles is roughly one hundred kilometres.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most frequent length mistake is mixing up the two gallons of the volume world, but in length the classic error is confusing feet and inches notation: 5'6" means five feet six inches, not five point six feet. Five point six feet is actually five feet seven inches, because 0.6 of a foot is 7.2 inches. Another common slip is converting a length and then forgetting to convert the unit on the answer — saying a room is 3.6 metres wide when you meant 3.6 yards, a difference of nearly thirty centimetres.

Square and cubic measures are a separate trap. A square foot is not twelve square inches — it is 144 square inches, because area scales with the square of the length factor. Similarly, a cubic foot contains 1,728 cubic inches. People also forget that a nautical mile is not the same as a land mile: the nautical mile is 1,852 metres and is used in aviation and shipping, while the statute mile is 1,609 metres. Always check which mile you are dealing with before converting a flight distance or a sailing route.

Tools and techniques for perfect results

For any conversion that matters, use a dedicated converter rather than mental arithmetic. Enter the value, choose the starting unit and the target unit, and let the tool apply the exact factor. Keep a note of the factor used so you can sanity-check the result: if you convert 100 miles and get 16 kilometres, you know something went wrong, because 100 miles should be about 161 kilometres. A good habit is to estimate first and then check — if the estimate and the tool agree within a few percent, the answer is almost certainly right.

When measuring physically, match your measuring tool to the job. A steel tape is best for room dimensions, a ruler for small objects, and a laser measure for long distances. Measure twice and write the unit next to every number, every time — a bare number without a unit is a mistake waiting to happen. In shared documents, agree on a single unit system before anyone starts measuring, and record conversions in both units where the audience might use either.

Frequently asked questions

How many centimetres are in an inch?
Exactly 25.4 centimetres are in ten inches; one inch is exactly 2.54 cm.

How do I convert feet to metres?
Multiply feet by 0.3048. Six feet is 1.83 metres.

What is 5 miles in kilometres?
Five miles is 8.05 kilometres — roughly eight kilometres.
