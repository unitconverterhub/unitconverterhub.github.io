MPG vs Litres per 100 km: Fuel Consumption Conversions Explained

UnitConverter guides · Measurement

Fuel economy is quoted in three different ways around the world, and two of them run in opposite directions. In the United States the figure is miles per gallon, where bigger is better. In most of Europe it is litres per 100 kilometres, where smaller is better. In Japan and parts of Asia it is kilometres per litre, where bigger is better again. Converting between them requires a reciprocal, not a simple multiplication, which is why so many people get it wrong.

Miles per gallon

Miles per gallon is the traditional fuel economy measure in the United States and the United Kingdom. It answers the question: how far can I drive on one gallon of fuel? A car that does 30 miles per gallon travels 30 miles on a gallon, and a car that does 50 travels 50 — bigger is better, and the scale is easy to understand. American cars typically achieve 25 to 35 miles per gallon, efficient hybrids reach 50 or more, and large trucks manage 6 to 8.

The trap is that the gallon is not the same in both countries that use miles per gallon. A US gallon is 3.785 litres and a UK gallon is 4.546 litres, so a car that does 30 miles per US gallon does 36 miles per UK gallon — the same car, the same fuel, a different number. When comparing a British car review with an American one, always check which gallon is meant. To convert miles per US gallon to miles per UK gallon, multiply by 1.201; to go the other way, divide by 1.201.

Litres per 100 kilometres

Litres per 100 kilometres is the standard in most of the world, and it answers a different question: how much fuel do I use to drive 100 kilometres? A car that uses 8 litres per 100 kilometres is moderately thirsty, a car that uses 5 is efficient, and a car that uses 12 is a gas guzzler. Smaller is better, which feels backwards to anyone used to miles per gallon, but the figure has a real advantage: it makes fuel costs easy to calculate, because you can multiply it directly by the distance and the fuel price.

The conversion between the two systems is a reciprocal. To convert miles per US gallon to litres per 100 kilometres, divide 235.21 by the miles per gallon figure. To convert litres per 100 kilometres to miles per US gallon, divide 235.21 by the litres figure. So 30 miles per gallon is 7.84 litres per 100 kilometres, and 8 litres per 100 kilometres is 29.4 miles per gallon. The constant 235.21 comes from the relationship between miles, gallons and kilometres, and it changes to 282.48 if you are using UK gallons.

Kilometres per litre

Kilometres per litre is used in Japan, India and several other countries, and it works like miles per gallon: bigger is better. A car that does 15 kilometres per litre is efficient, one that does 8 is thirsty. The conversion is simple: divide 100 by the litres per 100 kilometres figure to get kilometres per litre, and divide 100 by the kilometres per litre figure to get litres per 100 kilometres. A car that uses 6 litres per 100 kilometres does 16.7 kilometres per litre.

To convert kilometres per litre to miles per US gallon, multiply by 2.352. To convert miles per US gallon to kilometres per litre, divide by 2.352. These factors are exact enough for any practical purpose, and they make it easy to compare cars across markets. When shopping for a car abroad, convert the economy figure into the unit you think in before you compare — a car that looks efficient in litres per 100 kilometres can look ordinary in miles per gallon, and the conversion is the only way to tell.

What the numbers mean for your wallet

Fuel economy translates directly into money. A car that uses 8 litres per 100 kilometres and drives 15,000 kilometres a year uses 1,200 litres of fuel. At 1.50 per litre, that is 1,800 a year. A more efficient car at 5 litres per 100 kilometres uses 750 litres and costs 1,125 — a saving of 675 a year, every year. Over five years of ownership, the efficient car saves more than 3,000, which often outweighs a higher purchase price.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert MPG to L/100 km?
Divide 235.21 by the MPG figure (US gallons). 30 MPG is 7.84 L/100 km.

Is a bigger MPG always better?
Yes — more miles per gallon means more distance on the same fuel.

Why do UK and US MPG differ?
The UK gallon is 20 percent larger than the US gallon, so UK MPG figures are higher.
